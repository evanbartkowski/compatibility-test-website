# Direct questions, distinct decisions

The current focused library has 16 tests, 970 full-form questions and 184 short-form questions. Core definitions are v6; adult definitions are v3. Full forms contain 50?100 questions. Related topics are categories in a broader assessment rather than separate tests. See [the consolidation guide](FOCUSED_LIBRARY_AND_3D_PROFILE.md).

Questions name sex, kissing, foreplay, orgasm, sexual roles and aftercare when those are the subject. They do not substitute “private time” for sex. Relationship and personality questions describe a recognizable decision rather than ask the reader to endorse an attractive label. Consent, willingness, experience, limits and disclosure remain independent controls.

Examples from the live bank:

- Desire: “A partner asks directly, 'Do you want to have sex tonight?'”
- Aftercare: “After sex, receiving cuddling without another attempt to initiate sex.”
- Communication: “Hearing a partner talk about a sexual preference you have never asked for.”
- Fantasy: “Imagining a partner following your lead instead of keeping their usual sexual role.”
- Personality: “If friends book a trip around my availability, I check the dates before saying yes.”
- Choices: decide whether you want sex during the date, or talk about the possibility of sex before the date.

## Authorship and measurement

`src/data/assessments/focused.ts` publishes the consolidated banks from the preserved editorial sources in `candid/legacy/`. Each core category has ten situational statements, including two reverse-worded statements. Its short form samples two per category. Most adult categories have five distinct topics, each with separate receiving and offering questions; the fantasy catalog has ten per category. Receiving answers match only the corresponding offering answer. Binary choices measure preference direction separately from appeal.

Every live prompt is unique across the library, although a useful construct can appear from different angles in related assessments. Advice is generated from the revised categories and actual answers; it does not reinterpret an earlier response as an answer to a new question. The generated overview and fictional examples use the real registry and scorer.

## Historical answers

Original source definitions stay unchanged in the archive. New questions have new IDs, versioned draft storage and separate measurement cohorts. Existing invitations resolve their pinned definition. Old drafts remain in their original browser storage keys. They are never silently mapped onto different wording.

## Review rules

Name the actual behavior. Ask one useful decision. Give the reader a concrete setting or tradeoff. Keep the receive/offer perspectives accurate. Avoid lookalike questions whose only difference is a room, costume or decorative adjective. Keep skips available. A low preference is informative; there is no preferred personality or sexual profile to perform.
