# Focused assessment library

Updated November 20, 2026. The current library has **nine version 4.0.0 assessments**, with **650 authored statements**. Each has five equally represented categories and a **50-question standard form**, presented five questions per page. Full forms range from **50 to 100 questions**, with at least two opposite-worded questions per category. Estimates are about 12 minutes for standard forms and 12–24 minutes for full forms; actual time varies.

## Question quality and coverage

Questions describe concrete behavior, choices or preferences across ordinary life, uncertainty and pressure. Each standard category includes ten authored questions, with contrasting directions. Full forms add situations relevant to that category, rather than automated word substitutions. Every item contributes to exactly one declared category. Within each test, categories have equal question counts. Automated checks verify unique prompts, declared dimensions, complete definitions and scoring sensitivity for every item; editorial relevance still requires human judgment.

The model distinguishes preference from skill: an appetite for independent space, spontaneous desire, spoken affection or new experiences is not graded as a virtue. Skills such as communicating a limit are described as self-reported tendencies, not proof of safe behavior. There are no inferred diagnoses, attachment labels, invented ideal partners or sexual-interest labels from general agreement answers.

## Agreement and scoring contract

Seven labeled native radio tiles: Strongly disagree, Disagree, Somewhat disagree, Neither agree nor disagree, Somewhat agree, Agree, Strongly agree. Nothing is preselected. Number keys 1–7 and native arrow keys work within each focused question. A skipped statement contributes no value. There is no secondary importance rating in the curated forms.

Normalize a response with (value - 1) / 6 * 100. Invert opposite-worded statements before averaging their single declared category. Questions have equal weight within a category. Categories remain separate; the app does not manufacture an overall personality percentage. Scores are rounded for display. Definition versions and standard/full forms remain separate in history, partner reuse and participant cohorts.

Category readings use editorial bands: 0–39, 40–60 and 61–100. Each category has distinct lower, mixed and higher interpretations, plus a relevant tradeoff, practical experiment and reflection prompt. Reports expose actual answered/eligible counts and the statements contributing to the score. Fewer than three responses are explicitly tentative. Widely differing item contributions prompt a context note instead of concealing the difference behind an average. Missing categories remain unmeasured.

These are original exploratory self-reports, without clinical or psychometric validation. Editorial score bands are not diagnostic cutoffs or population norms. Agreement with an intimate statement never supplies consent.

## Comparisons and participant statistics

Partner comparisons use independently answered, version-compatible forms with explicit sharing controls. Category and item alignment is a descriptive product rubric, not a prediction of relationship success. Existing legacy matching strategies remain available to pinned historical reports.

Community statistics use actual opted-in participants, deduplicated by person and separated by definition, scoring engine and form. Category samples require at least 50 measured participants. Histograms use ten normalized bins; midrank percentiles and strict-below percentages are distinguished. Synthetic examples are explicitly labeled and never mixed into real cohorts. New version 4 cohorts begin independently of prior versions.
